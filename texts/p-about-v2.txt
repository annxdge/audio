About Studio Anaar.

Studio Anaar is a woman-led, collaborative, multidisciplinary practice working across web, brand, interiors, and editorial. The disciplines may seem unrelated at first, but I'm interested in what happens when they begin to inform one another: how an identity finds its way into a space, how an object communicates an idea, how the smallest decisions contribute to a much larger impression.

My approach brings together strategic thinking, visual research, and an appreciation for the particulars of making. I work with ideas, identities, digital experiences, and spaces, paying attention not only to how things look, but to what they communicate, how they function, and what makes them worth keeping around. There is already an extraordinary amount of visual information competing for our attention. I'm less interested in contributing to the pile than in making something with a reason to exist. Something considered, distinctive, and capable of holding its own without having to announce itself.

I collaborate with clients across different parts of the world, working in English, Spanish, and Portuguese. Each project begins with an attempt to understand what is actually needed, rather than simply what is being asked for. Clarity, accessibility, precision, and a certain suspicion of the disposable inform the process. I'm interested in work that can withstand a change of season, a shift in taste, or the arrival of the next big thing. Ideally, it should still make sense in five years.

A little about me.

I'm Ana Degenaar, the founder of Studio Anaar. I'm a digital designer, creative director, writer, and an interior designer in the making (the distinction is becoming increasingly technical).

I started the studio back when blogs were something people read rather than merely scrolled past, when discovering an interesting corner of the internet could occupy an entire morning. The internet has since become a rather different place, and my work has evolved alongside it. I've moved between disciplines, collected an unreasonable number of references, and become increasingly interested in the relationships between design, culture, and everyday life. What hasn't changed is my belief that making something simply because we can is not, in itself, a particularly compelling reason to make it.

Over the years, I've built websites, written for established online publications, and led creative projects helping brands articulate what they are, what they stand for, and how they might be understood by others. I'm currently completing my interior design studies, extending my interest in composition, proportion, materiality, and spatial experience beyond the screen. It turns out that many of the questions I ask about digital design become rather interesting when given four walls and a floor.

The blog.

The blog is part résumé, part notebook, part reference library, and part ongoing intellectual experiment. A place for following curiosities, collecting references, and investigating ideas that don't necessarily belong to the same conversation but might have more in common than initially suspected.

Its interests are deliberately difficult to contain within a single category. Architecture, design, objects, interiors, images, people, places, cultural history, visual language, and the occasional peculiar obsession all find their way here. Sometimes the starting point is a building; sometimes it's a drinking glass, a film still, an obscure design movement, or something noticed on an otherwise unremarkable afternoon. The subject matters, of course, but so does everything surrounding it: its history, its context, the circumstances of its creation, and the associations it brings to mind.

I'm interested in looking beyond the obvious function of things, in understanding why certain forms become familiar, why particular aesthetics endure, and how the objects and environments we encounter every day shape our perception of the world. Not everything needs a definitive conclusion. Some things are worth investigating simply to see where they lead.

Away from the studio, I can usually be found reading recipe books, sourcing interesting objects, writing poems, or looking for the best place on the beach to watch the sunset. I live in northeast Brazil, where the light changes the character of almost everything it touches. It's a rather good place to develop an interest in looking.
